YOUR LEARNING PATH

Four lessons, one big adventure.

Each lesson builds toward your own illustrated mini-story.

01
55 MINVOCABULARY · LISTENING

Meet the Cat!

Look at the cover, predict the story and discover the main characters.

“This is a cat.” · “The hat is red and white.”

Open lesson plan
  1. Warm-up · 10 min: Mystery hat. Guess what might be inside: “Is it a toy?”
  2. Before reading · 10 min: Look at the cover and predict using picture clues.
  3. Storytelling · 15 min: Read selected passages from a classroom copy; pause for predictions.
  4. Vocabulary game · 15 min: Point to the character or picture you hear.
  5. Exit ticket · 5 min: Draw a character and write “This is a ___. It is ___.”

Evidence: Recognizes characters and uses two target words.

🎩
02
55 MINGRAMMAR · SPEAKING

What Is Happening?

Use the present continuous to describe actions in pictures and mime games.

“The cat is jumping.” · “They are playing.”

Open lesson plan
  1. Act it out · 8 min: Mime jumping, running, dancing and cleaning.
  2. Grammar discovery · 12 min: Model subject + am/is/are + verb-ing.
  3. Picture detectives · 15 min: Ask and answer “What is the cat doing?”
  4. Human charades · 15 min: Guess actions and make a full sentence.
  5. Exit ticket · 5 min: Write two action sentences.

Evidence: Describes at least one action with support.

↗
03
55 MINREADING · WRITING

Build a Crazy Story!

Sequence events and plan a new adventure with a beginning, a problem and an ending.

“First…” · “Then…” · “Finally…”

Open lesson plan
  1. Story recap · 10 min: Put three or four teacher-selected picture cards in order.
  2. Model text · 10 min: Notice characters, actions and sequence words.
  3. Story map · 10 min: Choose characters, setting, action and ending.
  4. Writing workshop · 20 min: Write 3–5 original sentences and illustrate them.
  5. Peer feedback · 5 min: Check that the story has characters, actions and an ending.

Evidence: A sequenced story with at least two action sentences.

✎
04
55 MININTEGRATED SKILLS

Storybook Festival!

Finish your mini-book and share your adventure with classmates.

“My story is about…” · “Finally…”

Open lesson plan
  1. Guess the story · 5 min: Predict what happens from a drawing.
  2. Final production · 20 min: Complete three pages: characters, adventure, ending.
  3. Storytelling circle · 20 min: Present for about one minute; reading is allowed.
  4. Gallery walk · 5 min: View classmates’ stories and share a positive comment.
  5. Self-assessment · 5 min: Complete “I can…” statements.

Evidence: Presents a short illustrated story using familiar language.

YOUR TURN TO BE AN AUTHOR

Make a mini-story!

Invent your own silly adventure. Use the prompts to plan it, then write your sentences in your notebook.

  • Who is in your story?
  • Where does the adventure happen?
  • What are the characters doing?
  • How does the story end?

Try to use first, then and finally.

TEACHER CORNER

Assessment & inclusion

Look for progress, communication and confidence—not perfect English.

Assessment rubric

CriteriaWeightEvidence
Vocabulary & comprehension25%Identifies characters and key words.
Grammar & sentences25%Uses am/is/are + verb-ing.
Speaking & participation20%Shares ideas with available support.
Illustrated mini-story30%Creates a clear, sequenced story.

Make it inclusive

  • Visual supportUse pictures, gestures and word cards.
  • Sentence startersOffer “The cat is…” and “First…”.
  • Flexible responseAllow drawing, pointing, speaking or writing.
  • Challenge optionInvite confident learners to add adjectives and connectives.
Materials: classroom copy of the book, picture/word cards, paper, colored pencils, board and optional projector. Language focus: character and object vocabulary; adjectives; present continuous; first/then/finally.
